READ THE SMALL PRINT

Real idea. Illustrative numbers.

This is a simplified passenger-and-train simulation of selected Auckland corridors. It explains capacity constraints; it does not predict opening-day services or patronage.

VERIFIED The network change

CRL changes Waitematā (Britomart) from a terminal into a through station, links it with Maungawhau, and adds Te Waihorotiu and Karanga-a-Hape. CRL platforms allow for future nine-car trains. Longer trains here are a future scenario, not an opening-day promise.

AT describes East West, South City and Onehunga West services. This map abstracts East West and South City through-running; Onehunga, intermediate stops, freight, depots and the detailed track layout are omitted. Colours identify geographic corridors, not official service branding.

ESTIMATES Geography & journey time

Station positions are schematic. Each selected-station link takes 60–110 simulated seconds, an intentionally compressed estimate that keeps the experiment readable. This is not a journey planner.

SYNTHETIC Every operating number

Arrivals, destination weights, train capacities, target frequencies, headways, dwell, exchange speed, delays and junction clearance times are adjustable assumptions. Default old dispatch requests are 24 trainsets/hour; CRL requests 36, across represented services. Each trainset runs a return journey: old trains turn at Britomart, CRL trains pass through the city in both directions and return at the outer route end. Outer turnbacks are simplified to a normal dwell; fleets are unconstrained. Actual city passages are measured separately. Default capacity is 650 people per train in both networks. Five shared terminal platforms are represented at Britomart in the old model; other stops have one platform per direction.

Trains compete for directional track slots and station platforms. Old Britomart arrivals and departures share a throat clearance clock; terminal reversal holds a platform. New trains use separate directional slots and keep going. Newmarket and Maungawhau have shared junction clearance clocks. Passenger groups choose destinations, board trains that reduce their remaining route distance, transfer if needed, and queue when capacity runs out.

What the numbers mean

People/hour: completed journeys over the last 10 simulated minutes × 6; never a fixed capacity claim. City trains/hour: train passages at Waitematā over the same window; each directional passage counts, even if the same train later returns. Average wait: mean wait of passengers currently in station queues. Left behind: people missing their first available boarding in that window, counted once per journey. Occupancy: current people on trains ÷ active train capacity. Stress: the higher of queue pressure and the share of trains blocked.

Both networks start after a 20-minute warm-up. Reset repeats that seeded warm-up. Controls affect the next simulation tick; rolling metrics take time to settle. Demand at unopened CRL stations is reassigned to Waitematā, preserving total demand. Each experiment uses its own seeded runs of this same model.

Find the limit tests 0.5×–6× demand in 0.5× steps, with a 20-minute warm-up and 40-minute observation per trial. Overload means the final average queue exceeds five minutes of arrivals and has grown by more than two minutes of arrivals between the first and last 10-minute observation blocks. Reported sustainable capacity is completed journeys/hour at the last passing step, a coarse experimental estimate. If no step fails, the result is a lower bound. Transformation repeats this test for seven cumulative changes. The through-only stage is a counterfactual, unconstrained continuation and return beyond Britomart; no reversal or shared throat is charged. It is not a buildable plan. New stations redistribute existing city demand. Longer trains raise capacity from 650 to 900 in the default experiment; the final step reduces junction clearance from 55 to 40 seconds.
